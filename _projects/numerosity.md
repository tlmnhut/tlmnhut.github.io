PLACEHOLDER TEXT — rewrite before publishing.

People and many other animals judge "how many" quickly and without counting, with
a precision that falls off in a characteristic way as sets get larger. Whether an
artificial network trained on something else entirely picks up the same ability is
a useful test of what visual experience alone is sufficient to produce.

This line of work looks for number-selective units in networks that were never
asked to count, asks whether their tuning shows the ratio dependence that
characterises the human number sense, and compares the resulting representations
against behavioural and neural measures.
